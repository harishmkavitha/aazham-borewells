Overview

What we do

Whether it's a decades-old borewell that's slowed to a trickle or a new one that's throwing sand, our cleaning process follows the same disciplined sequence: understand the bore, clean it correctly, and prove the result.

Old borewells often need patient de-silting and care around ageing casing; new borewells usually need developing and sand clearing. We handle both.

What's included

Every cleaning process job covers

  • Inspection of bore and casing
  • Old-borewell de-silting
  • New-borewell development
  • Staged high-pressure flushing
  • Before-and-after yield check
  • Advice on keeping it clean

How the work runs

A clear process, start to finish

  1. Inspect

    We check depth, water level and casing condition to understand what we're dealing with.

  2. Plan the clean

    Old or new, silted or sandy — the bore's condition decides the method and the care needed.

  3. Flush in stages

    Staged high-pressure flushing lifts out silt, sand and debris without stressing the casing.

  4. Verify & advise

    We measure the restored yield and advise on how to keep the bore clean longer.

Questions

Frequently asked

Is cleaning an old borewell risky?

Old casings need care, which is exactly why we inspect first and choose a method that clears the bore without stressing ageing pipe.

Why does a new borewell need cleaning?

New bores often throw fine sand until they're properly developed. Developing and flushing settles this so your pump draws clean water.
